# Clippy island for macOS

The primary app lives in a small black island at the top of the screen. It has no normal app window or Dock icon. Rest the pointer on the green area for 1 second, or press **Control-Option-Space**, to open its compact composer. A tiny ring beside the pointer shows the hold filling up, and moving away cancels it. Clicking the island doesn't open it. While it starts, a white light sweeps down the left of the camera, under it and up the right. Once the local server has answered, the light settles on the right and turns into the green line, or a coral line with a message if something needs attention. On notched MacBooks, the collapsed island sits in the menu-bar row and at rest extends only 24 points to the right of the physical camera, showing a slowly pulsing vertical green line there, with nothing to its left. Green means the local server answers and the selected model is signed in. Pointing at the line wakes the island until the pointer leaves it: both sides grow to 24 points with curved shoulders, it hangs 10 points below the camera, and the line shrinks to a bead, glides under the camera and spreads into a soft 130-point line. While awake the line wanders left and right, then splits into two eyes that look toward the pointer and blink before flowing back together. Move the pointer off the island and its edge grabs a little: pure black liquid reaches up to 26 points toward it, with viscosity and gravity, then flows back when the pointer goes more than 46 points away, after 1 second, or on a click. Pixels are drawn only while there is liquid, so this costs nothing at rest. That liquid is drawn in a click-through panel, so it never blocks the menu bar. The waveform replaces the line, on a 36-point right side, only while audio is live (Talk, dictation or spoken replies). Progress, notices and problems show text left of the camera, up to 130 points, and a line that turns coral while working or when something is wrong; one-off notices return to the resting line after 5 seconds. No controls are placed behind the camera, and only the awake island hangs below it. Its top corners are square to meet the screen edge and its lower corners are rounded. Opening continuously expands the actual black island into a 480 × 200 curved shell below the camera. The curved backdrop stays joined to the camera cover and uses a translucent, sandblasted popover material that blurs the desktop behind the entire semicircle, with fine static grain. The frosted backdrop renders at 80% opacity for a 20% lighter surface; native blur and fine grain remain, while controls stay fully opaque. The expanded idle composer has no Ready label or duplicate green indicator; the compact menu-bar line remains its readiness cue. Progress, errors, and live audio still show their status. A subtle, light-weight caption at the top center shows the chat model and reasoning effort. Hover or click to choose a manual model or Auto; Auto delegates both model and effort to Decisions for each chat prompt. Draw settings are kept in a separate submenu and retain their existing choices; active status temporarily takes its place. When opening from another app with text selected, a small text icon and “12 words | selected text” appear beneath the input. The count is refreshed on each opening and cleared on close; it reads only the exposed Accessibility selection, without Copy or clipboard access. Secure fields and unsupported apps show no indicator. This is a local selection indicator, not an attachment: no selected text is retained or sent to a model. Existing Accessibility permission is required; hovering never prompts for it. The central typing area has no visible container, border, or placeholder: opening focuses the native field and shows its blinking cursor. Screen, Talk, spoken replies, chat model, Draw, More, and Collapse are small circular controls along its lower arc; hover anywhere in an option’s wedge for a soft green glow. Each control keeps its native tooltip and accessibility name; no duplicate descriptions appear beneath the typing area. The glow follows the pointer without activating any action; click the circle to activate it. Send is a thin white upward arrow inside a single fine circular outline, with no button fill; its 36-point hit area is preserved. Send/Return submits chat; the easel circle draws the current prompt with its independently selected Draw model; when the input is empty, it focuses the typing area. Closing reverses the same transition. Text and controls remain fully opaque above the transparent background. Reduce Motion skips these transitions. Displays without a notch use a floating pill with the same states, resting at 30 × 22. Click the rightmost circular chevron, press Escape while focused, or click outside to make it small again. After it opens, the pointer has to leave the island before a hold can open it again, so closing it with the pointer still there doesn't reopen it. While it rests, the camera area doesn't start a hold. Your unsent text stays in the composer. A quiet 0.6-second wake tone plays once when the pointer enters the idle green area, with a two-second cooldown and no looping. Toggle it independently of spoken replies using **… → Wake sound**; the setting persists across launches.

Build with `npm run desktop:build`, then open `build/Clippy.app`. The app reuses a healthy local server at `127.0.0.1:4317`. If none is available, it starts this project's server as its own child process, and stops only that child when you quit. No installation, login item or background service is registered. The build saves this checkout's Node/Codex paths in the app's `runtime.json`; rebuild after moving the checkout or changing those paths.

## Use

- Type a question and press Return, Send, or Draw. The native app calls the local Codex backend and progressively shows validated visual parts in a transparent floating drawing panel above the current desktop. No sample drawing appears automatically.
- Rotate, zoom or select 3D parts in the drawing. Follow-ups keep the previous scene and bounded conversation history in memory.
- Drag the drawing using its drag handle. Hide drawing dismisses both the drawing and captions. The … menu or menu-bar paperclip can reopen the last drawing.
- Captions float near the bottom of the display. Voice on/off toggles local macOS speech synthesis.
- Talk starts an OpenAI Realtime voice conversation. End or Escape stops the microphone and connection. Collapse keeps the voice session running, indicated by the island’s activity state. The speaker button mutes output, not the microphone. … → Dictate on-device remains available separately.
- Screen captures the main display once, with Clippy's panels temporarily hidden. It requests Screen Recording permission only when used. The screenshot remains in memory until sent or removed, and the temporary capture file is deleted immediately. Clicking Remove screen clears it. During Talk, Screen explicitly shares that single screenshot with the live voice session instead.
- Click the mascot circle along the composer's lower arc to switch directly between GPT and Claude, without a dropdown. Its icon, hover help, and model caption follow the selected provider: GPT uses the Codex SDK, and Claude uses your signed-in Claude CLI with no tools. Claude replaces everything the Codex SDK does: answers and drawings, selected-text editing, and Talk's drawings. No Claude API is used, and neither falls back to the other. Talk's live voice always stays on OpenAI's Realtime API. Hover over the top model caption for a quarter-second, or click it, to choose a drawing model and reasoning effort. The menu opens once per hover and leaves the draft intact. GPT offers Terra, Sol, Astra, and Luna; Claude offers Sonnet, Opus, and Haiku 5.5, each with Low through Maximum effort. Available account access is checked by the provider; failures stay visible. Each provider remembers its model and each model remembers its effort across launches. These choices apply to prompts, visual follow-ups, and Talk’s drawings; the live voice model and selected-text editing models keep their own settings. Draw is available for either model.
- GPT drawings always request Fast automatically; there is no Standard/Fast setting in the notch app. Your account must support Fast; errors remain visible and never trigger a silent fallback. Claude keeps its own model settings.
- Cancel aborts the active model request and restores the last completed visual. Escape also cancels an active request, or otherwise hides drawings and collapses the island. … → New conversation clears the in-memory conversation and attachment.

## Selected-text editing

Select text in another app, then press **Control-Option-D** to proofread or **Control-D** to rewrite. Both use the selected model at high effort, **GPT-6.1 Sol** or **Claude Sonnet 5.5**, independent of the drawing settings. The result replaces the selection automatically using normal Paste; Command-Z undoes it. Clippy never presses Return or submits the field. Control-D is reserved globally while Clippy runs, overriding apps that use it for forward-delete or EOF.

Use the paperclip menu → Enable text editing… to open the permission prompt. Allow Clippy under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility on first use. Fn dictation is untouched. Only selected text is sent, up to 12,000 characters. Password fields and fields that expose no verifiable selection are excluded. If you change the field, app, selected range or selected text before completion, Clippy leaves it untouched and shows a copyable result. The clipboard is restored after Copy/Paste unless it changed meanwhile. Use the paperclip menu → Cancel text edit to stop a request.

## Boundaries and verification

This is an independent implementation, not a modified HeyClicky app. The native bridge and drawing navigation accept only the local server origin. Model results are validated by the backend before being drawn. Local screen capture and on-device voice are explicitly activated by you.

The build uses native Swift/AppKit, WKWebView, Carbon hotkeys, Speech and AVFoundation. It is ad-hoc signed for this Mac, not notarized for distribution. Microphone and speech availability depend on the Mac, language and permissions. Screen Recording permission can require restarting the app after authorization. Empty transparent drawing areas pass clicks through; objects and controls receive clicks. Command-D focuses the last drawing while Clippy is active, and Command-Q quits.

Compilation, plist syntax and signature checks are performed by the build script. Actual native rendering, microphone, screen capture and hotkey behavior require interactive verification separately.

Run `bash scripts/test-island-layout.sh` on macOS to check notch clearance, camera-width alignment, external-display coordinates and the safe-area fallback. The expanded notch background never receives mouse events. At rest the collapsed island occupies 24 points to the right of the camera; while awake it occupies 24 points on each side and can cover menu items in that space. Hardware-notch appearance needs a physical-screen check; software screenshots do not capture the camera cutout.

### Native composer checks

Rest the pointer on the green area for 1 second to open the composer, type a draft, collapse with the chevron, then reopen and confirm the draft remains. Verify that Draw focuses empty input and sends a typed prompt, toggle the Model circle in both directions and check the More menu, and press Escape while the prompt is focused to collapse. Verify the borderless input, thin white send arrow with a circular outline, and frosted expanded backdrop (background text should blur while controls stay crisp), then watch it return to the compact island while closing. Quickly reverse direction and confirm there is no reset to a thin strip or stale animation endpoint. With Reduce Motion enabled, both transitions should complete immediately. Native layout tests cover control containment, spacing, the non-overlapping camera cover and continuous/reversible transition geometry; microphone, screen sharing and live AI requests require separate permission and service checks.

### Living composer signal

The expanded semicircle keeps a slim green signal above the model caption. It inherits the compact notch's spring state and can drift, split into pointer-following blinking eyes, or morph into a gentle wave. Motion runs locally at 30 fps while expanded, stops on collapse, and becomes a static line with Reduce Motion. It is decorative and does not intercept input.

On opening or a coarse activity-state change, the native app asynchronously requests a pattern from `/api/signal`. Decisions (`gpt-6-luna`) chooses only `line`, `eyes`, or `wave`; no generated code or coordinates execute. Only `ready`, `working`, `speaking`, or `attention` leaves the backend—no draft, selection, screenshot, or conversation. The endpoint requires the local session token, rejects extra fields, bounds input to 128 bytes, and caps Decisions to one request per 30 seconds with a 2.5-second timeout. Rapid reopening reuses the last selection; missing access, refusal or offline failures keep local motion. API use is separately billed through the existing approved key.

Verify: hover open and check the signal above the model caption; move the pointer to see eyes follow, wait for a blink, collapse/reopen, and check that typing and the model menu remain usable. With macOS Reduce Motion enabled the strip stays still. This strip does not imply human emotions or reveal model reasoning.
