# Clippy island for macOS

The primary app lives in a small black island at the top of the screen. It has no normal app window or Dock icon. Click the green area, or press **Control-Option-Space**, to open its compact composer. On notched MacBooks, the collapsed island sits in the menu-bar row and at rest extends only 24 points to the right of the physical camera, showing a slowly pulsing vertical green line there, with nothing to its left. Green means the local server answers and the selected model is signed in. Pointing at the line wakes the island until the pointer leaves it: both sides grow to 24 points with curved shoulders, it hangs 10 points below the camera, and the line shrinks to a bead, glides under the camera and spreads into a soft 130-point line. While awake the line wanders left and right, then splits into two eyes that look toward the pointer and blink before flowing back together. Move the pointer off the island and its edge grabs a little: pure black liquid reaches up to 26 points toward it, with viscosity and gravity, then flows back when the pointer goes more than 46 points away, after 1 second, or on a click elsewhere. A click on the liquid opens the composer. Pixels are drawn only while there is liquid, so this costs nothing at rest. That liquid is drawn in a panel that only takes clicks when the pointer is on it, so it never blocks the menu bar. The waveform replaces the line, on a 36-point right side, only while audio is live (Talk, dictation or spoken replies). Progress, notices and problems show text left of the camera, up to 130 points, and a line that turns coral while working or when something is wrong; one-off notices return to the resting line after 5 seconds. No controls are placed behind the camera, and only the awake island hangs below it. Its top corners are square to meet the screen edge and its lower corners are rounded. Opening continuously expands the actual black island into a 440 × 176 glass bubble below the camera. Its black clears from bottom to top while the corners round and the controls appear. Closing runs the same material transition in reverse, reforming the black before settling back into the compact island. The black camera cover stops at the glass edge so no rectangle shows through it. The expanded surface uses the regular native Liquid Glass material on macOS 26 and newer to soften background text, with a visual-effect material fallback on older systems. Its padded prompt, circular blue Send button and translucent accessory controls preserve the same actions. Reduce Motion skips these transitions. Displays without a notch use a floating pill with the same states, resting at 30 × 22. Click the top-right chevron, press Escape while focused, or click outside to make it small again. Hovering never opens the composer; only a click on the green area or the shortcut does. While it rests, clicking the camera area does nothing. Your unsent text stays in the composer. A quiet 0.6-second wake tone plays once when the pointer enters the idle green area, with a two-second cooldown and no looping. Toggle it independently of spoken replies using **… → Wake sound**; the setting persists across launches.

Build with `npm run desktop:build`, then open `build/Clippy.app`. The app reuses a healthy local server at `127.0.0.1:4317`. If none is available, it starts this project's server as its own child process, and stops only that child when you quit. No installation, login item or background service is registered. The build saves this checkout's Node/Codex paths in the app's `runtime.json`; rebuild after moving the checkout or changing those paths.

## Use

- Type a question and press Return or Send. The native app calls the local Codex backend and progressively shows validated visual parts in a transparent floating drawing panel above the current desktop. No sample drawing appears automatically.
- Rotate, zoom or select 3D parts in the drawing. Follow-ups keep the previous scene and bounded conversation history in memory.
- Drag the drawing using its drag handle. Hide drawing dismisses both the drawing and captions. The … menu or menu-bar paperclip can reopen the last drawing.
- Captions float near the bottom of the display. Voice on/off toggles local macOS speech synthesis.
- Talk starts an OpenAI Realtime voice conversation. End or Escape stops the microphone and connection. Collapse keeps the voice session running, indicated by the island’s activity state. The speaker button mutes output, not the microphone. … → Dictate on-device remains available separately.
- Screen captures the main display once, with Clippy's panels temporarily hidden. It requests Screen Recording permission only when used. The screenshot remains in memory until sent or removed, and the temporary capture file is deleted immediately. Clicking Remove screen clears it. During Talk, Screen explicitly shares that single screenshot with the live voice session instead.
- Composer questions can go to GPT through the Codex SDK or to Claude (Sonnet 5.5, low effort) through your signed-in Claude CLI, with no tools and no fallback between them. Switch with the GPT/Claude toggle at the top of the composer; Standard/Fast only applies to GPT, and the choice is remembered across launches. Selected-text editing and Talk stay on GPT.
- Standard/Fast selects the requested Codex service tier. Your account must support the selected tier; errors remain visible and never trigger a silent fallback.
- Cancel aborts the active model request and restores the last completed visual. Escape also cancels an active request, or otherwise hides drawings and collapses the island. … → New conversation clears the in-memory conversation and attachment.

## Selected-text editing

Select text in another app, then press **Control-Option-D** to proofread or **Control-D** to rewrite. Both always use **GPT-6.1 Sol with high reasoning**, independent of the Terra/Fast drawing settings. The result replaces the selection automatically using normal Paste; Command-Z undoes it. Clippy never presses Return or submits the field. Control-D is reserved globally while Clippy runs, overriding apps that use it for forward-delete or EOF.

Use the paperclip menu → Enable text editing… to open the permission prompt. Allow Clippy under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility on first use. Fn dictation is untouched. Only selected text is sent, up to 12,000 characters. Password fields and fields that expose no verifiable selection are excluded. If you change the field, app, selected range or selected text before completion, Clippy leaves it untouched and shows a copyable result. The clipboard is restored after Copy/Paste unless it changed meanwhile. Use the paperclip menu → Cancel text edit to stop a request.

## Boundaries and verification

This is an independent implementation, not a modified HeyClicky app. The native bridge and drawing navigation accept only the local server origin. Model results are validated by the backend before being drawn. Local screen capture and on-device voice are explicitly activated by you.

The build uses native Swift/AppKit, WKWebView, Carbon hotkeys, Speech and AVFoundation. It is ad-hoc signed for this Mac, not notarized for distribution. Microphone and speech availability depend on the Mac, language and permissions. Screen Recording permission can require restarting the app after authorization. Empty transparent drawing areas pass clicks through; objects and controls receive clicks. Command-D focuses the last drawing while Clippy is active, and Command-Q quits.

Compilation, plist syntax and signature checks are performed by the build script. Actual native rendering, microphone, screen capture and hotkey behavior require interactive verification separately.

Run `bash scripts/test-island-layout.sh` on macOS to check notch clearance, camera-width alignment, external-display coordinates and the safe-area fallback. The expanded notch background never receives mouse events. At rest the collapsed island occupies 24 points to the right of the camera; while awake it occupies 24 points on each side and can cover menu items in that space. Hardware-notch appearance needs a physical-screen check; software screenshots do not capture the camera cutout.

### Native composer checks

Click the green area to open the composer, type a draft, collapse with the chevron, then reopen and confirm the draft remains. Verify the tier and More menus, and press Escape while the prompt is focused to collapse. Watch the black surface expand and clear into glass, then reform into black while closing. Quickly reverse direction and confirm there is no reset to a thin strip or stale animation endpoint. With Reduce Motion enabled, both transitions should complete immediately. Native layout tests cover control containment, spacing, the non-overlapping camera cover and continuous/reversible transition geometry; microphone, screen sharing and live AI requests require separate permission and service checks.
