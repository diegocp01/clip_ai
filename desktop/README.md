# Clip island for macOS

The primary app lives in a small black island at the top of the screen. It has no normal app window or Dock icon. Rest the pointer on the green area for 1 second, or press **Control-Option-Space**, to open its compact composer. A tiny ring beside the pointer shows the hold filling up, and moving away cancels it. Clicking the island doesn't open it. The startup white light shares the green horizontal signal’s bright core, translucent shoulders, fading tips and soft halo. Its route and timing are unchanged. While it starts, a white light sweeps down the left of the camera, under it and up the right. Once the local server has answered, the light settles on the right and turns into the green line, or a coral line with a message if something needs attention. On notched MacBooks, the collapsed island sits in the menu-bar row and at rest reserves 48 points inside its left black side for the habit ring and extends 24 points to the right of the physical camera for the slowly pulsing vertical green line. Green means the local server answers and the selected model is signed in. Pointing at the line wakes the island until the pointer leaves it: both signal shoulders grow by 24 points beside the reserved habit area, it hangs 10 points below the camera, and the line shrinks to a bead, glides under the camera and spreads into a soft 130-point line. While awake the line moves left and right, then splits into two eyes that look toward the pointer and blink, all within the first second, before flowing back together if the pointer stays. A faint light follows the pointer inside the island. Moving the pointer off the island returns it to rest. The waveform replaces the line, on a 36-point right side, only while audio is live (Talk, dictation or spoken replies). Progress, notices and problems show text left of the camera, up to 130 points, and a line that turns coral while working or when something is wrong; one-off notices return to the resting line after 5 seconds. No controls are placed behind the camera, and only the awake island hangs below it. Its top corners are square to meet the screen edge and its lower corners are rounded. Opening continuously expands the actual black island into a 480 × 200 curved shell below the camera. The curved backdrop stays joined to the camera cover and uses a translucent, sandblasted HUD material that blurs the desktop behind the entire semicircle, with fine static grain. The native material remains at full view opacity to preserve blur; a separate light tint and fine grain provide the finish, while controls stay fully opaque. The expanded idle composer has no Ready label or duplicate green indicator; the compact menu-bar line remains its readiness cue. Progress, errors, and live audio still show their status. A subtle, light-weight caption at the top center shows the chat model and reasoning effort. Hover or click to choose a manual model or Auto; Auto delegates both model and effort to Decisions for each chat prompt. Draw settings are kept in a separate submenu and retain their existing choices; active status temporarily takes its place. When opening from another app with text selected, a small text icon and “12 words | selected text” appear beneath the input. The count is refreshed on each opening and cleared on close; it reads only the exposed Accessibility selection, without Copy or clipboard access. Secure fields and unsupported apps show no indicator. On a user opening, the captured selection is sent to Decisions to choose proofreading or rewriting, then automatically edited; no typed prompt is required. The original app keeps keyboard focus, and normal Paste replaces only an unchanged writable selection with Undo available. A changed/read-only selection gets a copyable result; repeated opening of the same selection does not repeat the edit. Close or Cancel stops the request. Existing Accessibility permission is required; hovering never prompts for it. The central typing area has no visible container, border, or placeholder: opening without a selection focuses the native field and shows its blinking cursor. Screen, Talk, spoken replies, chat model, Draw, More, and Collapse are small circular controls along its lower arc; hover anywhere in an option’s wedge for a soft green glow. Each control keeps its native tooltip and accessibility name; no duplicate descriptions appear beneath the typing area. The glow follows the pointer without activating any action; click the circle to activate it. Send is a thin white upward arrow inside a single fine circular outline, with no button fill; its 36-point hit area is preserved. Send/Return asks Decisions whether a visual would help: yes uses independent Draw settings, no uses chat settings (manual or Auto); unavailable format routing falls back to text. The easel circle always draws the current prompt with its independently selected Draw model; when the input is empty, it focuses the typing area. Closing reverses the same transition. Text and controls remain fully opaque above the transparent background. Reduce Motion skips these transitions. Displays without a notch use a floating pill with the same states, resting at 78 × 22 including the reserved habit area. Click the rightmost circular chevron, press Escape while focused, or click outside to make it small again. After it opens, the pointer has to leave the island before a hold can open it again, so closing it with the pointer still there doesn't reopen it. While it rests, the camera area doesn't start a hold. Your unsent text stays in the composer. A quiet 0.6-second wake tone plays once when the pointer enters the idle green area, with a two-second cooldown and no looping. Toggle it independently of spoken replies using **… → Wake sound**; the setting persists across launches.

Build with `npm run desktop:build`, then open `build/Clip.app`. The app reuses a healthy local server at `127.0.0.1:4317`. If none is available, it starts this project's server as its own child process, and stops only that child when you quit. No installation, login item or background service is registered. The build saves this checkout's Node/Codex paths in the app's `runtime.json`; rebuild after moving the checkout or changing those paths.

## Use

- Type a question and press Return, Send, or Draw. The native app calls the local Codex backend and progressively shows validated visual parts in a transparent floating drawing panel above the current desktop. No sample drawing appears automatically.
- Rotate, zoom or select 3D parts in the drawing. Follow-ups keep the previous scene and bounded conversation history in memory.
- Drag the drawing using its drag handle. While a drawing, reply or comparison is on screen, a small floating X appears right under the notch, or under the composer when it's open. It closes all of them at once, as does Hide drawing. The … menu or menu-bar paperclip can reopen the last drawing.
- Captions float near the bottom of the display. Voice on/off toggles local macOS speech synthesis.
- While explaining a visual during Talk, the voice can use a red pointer with a fading tail to point at or circle parts of a 3D model, diagram or HTML panel, write short notes on them, and rotate or zoom a 3D model. It's drawn on the visual only: your real mouse isn't touched, and it can't click anything. Speaking or grabbing the drawing stops it. Open `/overlay.html?demo=water&cursor=1` or `/overlay.html?demo=html&cursor=1` on the local server to preview it.
- Talk starts an OpenAI Realtime voice conversation. End or Escape stops the microphone and connection. Collapse keeps the voice session running, indicated by the island’s activity state. The speaker button mutes output, not the microphone. … → Dictate on-device remains available separately.
- Screen captures the main display once, with Clip's panels temporarily hidden. It requests Screen Recording permission only when used. The screenshot remains in memory until sent or removed, and the temporary capture file is deleted immediately. Clicking Remove screen clears it. During Talk, Screen explicitly shares that single screenshot with the live voice session instead.
- Click the provider circle for **Codex · Claude · Both**. Codex and Claude choose the chat and selected-text editing provider. Both sends the same text and optional image to both providers and shows two copyable text answers in a separate glass comparison window; it never replaces selected text automatically. Both is text-only: it does not run automatic Draw routing, produce drawings, or speak its answers. The Draw button and Talk retain their independent single-provider settings. Hover or click the model caption to choose models and reasoning effort; Both offers separate Codex and Claude submenus. Auto leaves Both and returns to single-model Decisions routing. Each provider remembers its model and effort. Codex uses the signed-in Codex SDK and Claude uses the signed-in Claude CLI, with tools disabled. Neither silently falls back to the other.
- OpenAI Draw defaults to **GPT-6.1 Sol · Low · Ultrafast** through the paid OpenAI Responses API. You can still choose a different Draw model and effort in the Draw settings submenu; Terra uses Fast. Typed Draw, automatic visuals, voice tools and visual follow-ups share this API transport. The response records the actual served tier; API failures never fall back to a subscription request. Claude keeps its own model settings and CLI account.
- Cancel aborts the active model request and restores the last completed visual. Escape also cancels an active request, or otherwise hides drawings and collapses the island. … → New conversation clears the in-memory conversation and attachment.

## Selected-text editing

Opening the notch with selected text automatically chooses proofreading or rewriting through Decisions (minimal proofreading if the decision is unavailable). To choose manually, select text in another app, then press **Control-Option-D** to proofread or **Control-D** to rewrite. Both use the selected model at high effort, **GPT-6.1 Sol** or **Claude Sonnet 5.5**, independent of the drawing settings. The result replaces the selection automatically using normal Paste; Command-Z undoes it. Clip never presses Return or submits the field. Control-D is reserved globally while Clip runs, overriding apps that use it for forward-delete or EOF.

Use the paperclip menu → Enable text editing… to open the permission prompt. Allow Clip under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility on first use. Fn dictation is untouched. Only selected text is sent, up to 12,000 characters. Password fields and fields that expose no verifiable selection are excluded. If you change the field, app, selected range or selected text before completion, Clip leaves it untouched and shows a copyable result. The clipboard is restored after Copy/Paste unless it changed meanwhile. Use the paperclip menu → Cancel text edit to stop a request.

## Boundaries and verification

This is an independent implementation, not a modified HeyClicky app. The native bridge and drawing navigation accept only the local server origin. Model results are validated by the backend before being drawn. Screen context is captured only after a submitted prompt or a voice tool request, or with the manual Screen button; there is no continuous screen watching. On-device dictation is explicitly activated by you.

The build uses native Swift/AppKit, WKWebView, Carbon hotkeys, Speech and AVFoundation. It is ad-hoc signed for this Mac, not notarized for distribution. Microphone and speech availability depend on the Mac, language and permissions. Screen Recording permission can require restarting the app after authorization. Empty transparent drawing areas pass clicks through; objects and controls receive clicks. Command-D focuses the last drawing while Clip is active, and Command-Q quits.

Compilation, plist syntax and signature checks are performed by the build script. Actual native rendering, microphone, screen capture and hotkey behavior require interactive verification separately.

Run `bash scripts/test-island-layout.sh` on macOS to check notch clearance, camera-width alignment, external-display coordinates and the safe-area fallback. The expanded notch background never receives mouse events. At rest the collapsed island occupies 24 points to the right of the camera; while awake it occupies 24 points on each side and can cover menu items in that space. Hardware-notch appearance needs a physical-screen check; software screenshots do not capture the camera cutout.

### Native composer checks

Rest the pointer on the green area for 1 second to open the composer, type a draft, collapse with the chevron, then reopen and confirm the draft remains. Verify that Draw focuses empty input and sends a typed prompt, toggle the Model circle in both directions and check the More menu, and press Escape while the prompt is focused to collapse. Verify the borderless input, thin white send arrow with a circular outline, and frosted expanded backdrop (background text should blur while controls stay crisp), then watch it return to the compact island while closing. Quickly reverse direction and confirm there is no reset to a thin strip or stale animation endpoint. With Reduce Motion enabled, both transitions should complete immediately. Native layout tests cover control containment, spacing, the non-overlapping camera cover and continuous/reversible transition geometry; microphone, screen sharing and live AI requests require separate permission and service checks.

### Living composer signal

The expanded semicircle keeps a slim green signal above the model caption. It inherits the compact notch's spring state and can drift, split into blinking eyes, or morph into a gentle wave. Shape and mouse attention are separate: Follow reacts to horizontal and vertical pointer movement anywhere within the curved semicircle, then wanders when the pointer leaves. Wander, Glide Left, and Glide Right ignore the mouse and move independently. Switching modes eases through local springs rather than snapping. Motion runs locally at 30 fps while expanded, stops on collapse, and becomes a static line with Reduce Motion. It is decorative and does not intercept input.

On opening or a coarse activity-state change, the native app asynchronously requests a pattern from `/api/signal`. Decisions (`gpt-6-luna`) chooses `line`, `eyes`, or `wave` plus a motion policy: `follow`, `wander`, `drift_left`, or `drift_right`. Only these validated presets execute; no generated code or coordinates run. Both answers must be valid or the last safe behavior stays in place. Only `ready`, `working`, `speaking`, or `attention` leaves the backend—no draft, selection, screenshot, or conversation. The endpoint requires the local session token, rejects extra fields, bounds input to 128 bytes, and caps Decisions to one request per 30 seconds with a 2.5-second timeout. Rapid reopening reuses the last selection; missing access, refusal or offline failures keep local motion. API use is separately billed through the existing approved key.

Verify: hover open and check the signal above the model caption; in Follow mode move anywhere inside the semicircle and check the signal follows; in an independent mode verify opposing pointer movement does not change its path; wait for a blink, collapse/reopen, and check that typing and the model menu remain usable. With macOS Reduce Motion enabled the strip stays still. This strip does not imply human emotions or reveal model reasoning.

The frosted composer uses an active behind-window HUD material at full view opacity. Its translucency comes from AppKit's material; the separate black tint is only 8% (with an 80% finish multiplier) plus fine grain. Do not reduce the material or an ancestor's alpha to adjust transparency: that mixes sharp background detail back into the blur. The petroleum cover and independent finish still drive the opening/closing morph. macOS accessibility transparency settings remain respected.

The half circle has a pure black center around the typing area, centered on the camera. It fades out just before the divider lines begin, so the ring holding the lines and controls keeps the gray frosted material out to the border. The black is clipped to the shell, stays behind the controls, and also appears on screens without a notch. The native blur retains full opacity.

The app is branded Clip and builds as `build/Clip.app`. Its bundle identifier remains `local.clippy.codex` so existing preferences and permission identity are retained. Internal `clippy` WebKit messages and session headers are compatibility interfaces, not displayed branding.

### Visual explanations during Talk

Ask “How do molecules work?” in Talk: the Realtime voice can call Draw without a separate drawing command, using the current Draw provider/model/effort. The existing desktop overlay receives the progressive visual and the voice explains it after the tool confirms it is shown. Ask for “voice only” to avoid a drawing. Greetings/simple facts stay verbal, drawing failures get a verbal fallback, and interrupted responses cannot start a stale drawing call. End stops microphone/audio and pending drawing work.

### Automatic screenshots

Send/Draw first asks Decisions whether the submitted prompt needs the visible screen. An attached image bypasses that check; missing/failed/uncertain decisions continue without capturing. Screen-specific requests capture the main display once after hiding Clip’s panels. The small **Screenshot captured** line sits below the input and selected-text badge. **Screenshot unavailable** indicates missing access or a failed capture; automatic requests never open a permission prompt. Click Screen to request Screen Recording access manually.

Talk can call `capture_screen` for screen-dependent questions (one per spoken turn, eight per session), then discuss the image or pass it to Draw. It waits for capture before drawing if both tools are requested together. End prevents late image delivery and clears local voice image state. Typed Stop cancels preflight and prevents late delivery; closing the composer does not reopen it after capture. Temporary image files are removed. Ordinary typed chat/Draw models receive preflight context but do not gain general computer-control tools.

Verify with a disposable page: send “What does this error on my screen mean?” and confirm the screenshot line appears before the answer. Ask “How do molecules work?” and confirm there is no capture. Repeat in Talk, then End during capture; no late image should be shared. Screen Recording permission and actual voice playback need native verification separately from the automated tests.

### Compare with Both

Choose the provider circle → Both, then send a question or attach a screenshot and ask about it. The native comparison window shows Codex and Claude independently, including each model/effort, a selectable full answer, and separate Copy buttons. Stop or closing a running comparison cancels both requests. A provider error stays in its own column while the other can finish. A screenshot taken for Both is shared with both providers; Clip hides the comparison window as well as its other panels during capture.

For proofreading, choose Both before selecting text in another app and opening the notch, or use the existing proofreading/rewriting shortcuts. Decisions chooses one edit action, then both models edit the exact same selection. The original text remains untouched and either alternative can be copied. These editing calls keep the existing fixed editing models/efforts. Each chat comparison is independent: answers from one provider are never fed into the other, and previous comparison results are not included in the next prompt.

### Floating reply bubbles

Draw now makes a separate Decisions API choice between a physical 3D model and a live HTML teaching panel. Airplanes, molecules, machines and spatial structures favor 3D; code, worked examples, quizzes and tables favor HTML. A failed/timed-out format decision falls back to 3D. The selected Draw provider/model/effort handles generation after that choice. 3D results require actual model parts and cannot substitute diagram boxes or side cards. HTML keeps the existing script-free sandbox and bounded progressive sections. Native/voice requests use `/api/ask`; the older browser workspace endpoint remains compatible.

After Draw completes, **Continue** appears below its text reply. It starts Realtime voice with the original question, answer, visual summary and named cursor targets, keeps the current visual, and enables the red guide cursor with its fading tail. The guide can point, circle, write, rotate/zoom 3D, or reveal/scroll HTML answers; you can interrupt and ask follow-up questions. This guide acts only inside Clip's visual, and does not turn on screen capture or Point & Share. End Talk stops its microphone and guide as usual. Verify with “Visualize how airplanes work” (recognizable 3D airplane), a next-token quiz (HTML), Continue on each, a spoken follow-up, interruption, End, and cancelled generation while switching formats.

Single replies appear once in a minimal floating frosted bubble directly below the semicircle, with selectable, scrollable full text and a quiet Copy control. Sending a prompt keeps the composer open; the bubble follows it when expanded or collapsed. A title alone (for example “Hello”) never opens a second drawing overlay, and a text-only result clears the previous visual. The full displayed string feeds OpenAI spoken replies when voice is enabled. The single dismiss control sits below the bubble. Dismissing stops speech; during Talk it hides the bubble until the next voice response without ending the microphone. Both shows two compact provider-labeled bubbles and remains silent.

Realtime captions follow audio transcript delta/done events and the completed audio transcript, not the drawing model’s reply. Draw rendering cannot overwrite a live voice caption. The old 500-character native and 1,500-character bridge truncation is removed. On interruption the old transcript is cleared because audio timing cannot provide a precise heard-word transcript; a final transcript may arrive ahead of playback. Check a long reply (>500 characters), scroll to its end and copy it; check Talk drawing while it speaks, then interrupt it and confirm an old caption does not reappear.

### Opening greeting

Each fresh opening briefly replaces the idle model caption with a short greeting (at most five words). A shuffled local set avoids repeats until its 24 lines are used, remembers the last line across launches, and works offline without sending any user context. It holds for 3.2 seconds, fades out over 1.2 seconds, then fades the current model selector back in over 0.5 seconds. Reduced Motion uses a simple timed replacement. Clicking opens model settings immediately; automatic hover menus wait until the greeting is gone. Closing cancels the timer, and reopening starts a fresh line. Repositioning an already-open composer does not restart it. Work and voice status retain their existing priority.

### Voice-reactive compact notch

During Realtime Talk, your microphone makes the green line/eyes shimmer; Clip’s audible replies use the white waveform. Separate input/output levels keep the speakers distinct, and detected user speech takes priority when interrupting a reply. The compact black shell follows the stronger of the two measured levels. In the expanded composer, microphone energy also gently brightens the green signal at the top. The shell grows up to 20 points wider and 4 points deeper, keeps its top pinned, and eases back during silence. Expanded composer controls stay still. Reduce Motion keeps the shell and voice bars static. Muted reply audio does not drive the effect; microphone input still can. Missing or stale levels settle to zero.

Metering reuses the existing voice streams, requests no extra microphone access, and sends only normalized input/output levels and the existing speech-detected flag to the native UI at 30 Hz. Raw samples stay local and are not retained. Ending Talk closes the meter, audio graph and native timer. Verify on a Mac by speaking softly/loudly, listening to a reply, muting replies, expanding/collapsing, enabling Reduce Motion and ending Talk; check the physical camera clearance as well as a non-notched display.

### Point & Share during Talk

Start Talk, then click the custom comet-pointer circle in the bottom-center slot (the chat-provider selector returns when Talk ends). Its red state and the “Point sharing” notice confirm sharing is active. Move around or circle what you mean while asking your question: a click-through red comet and a three-second trail follow the normal system pointer, which remains available for normal app interaction. Click the same circle again or End Talk to stop. Point & Share never starts automatically.

Motion samples stay in memory, at up to 30 Hz and 90 recent points. After movement, Clip captures a region up to 760×520 points on the display under the pointer, adds the red trail, and shares it with the current OpenAI Realtime session. Captures wait at least 2.5 seconds apart, prefer a brief pause, and have a 120-frame limit per Talk session. No stationary repeats, permanent recordings, or queue of pending uploads are created. Temporary capture files are removed after reading. Crop pixels can include nearby content within that region. The current screenshot notice confirms each sent frame. Only the latest three pointer-image items remain in the voice conversation; deletion is conversation-context management, not a claim about provider data retention. Frames alone do not trigger speech. A full-screen capture tool request while this mode is enabled reuses the latest available crop instead.

Stopping, ending Talk, connection failure, lock/session-inactive notification, display sleep, or display reconfiguration cancels the mode and prevents late uploads. Existing Screen Recording permission is required. Leaving the notch open is optional; its sharing notice is visible when expanded and the red marker identifies active sharing elsewhere. The model receives bounded snapshots and trail coordinates, not a continuous video or access to control the cursor. Drawing can use the latest shared crop as context.

Validation: verify with a disposable page containing several labeled objects, enable Talk → Point & Share, circle one label and ask which one is circled. Check the red tail, screenshot-sent notice, normal click-through, second-monitor crop, and immediate stop when toggled off or Talk ends. Physical pointer/microphone and screen-lock behavior require an unlocked Mac; compilation and synthetic image checks alone do not verify those paths.

### Live HTML explanations during Talk

With Talk connected, enable the comet-pointer circle and circle the relevant area.
Ask “Explain this with an example and a question I can answer.” Realtime can now
call `show_explanation` for a floating HTML panel, or `draw_visual` for a 3D/diagram
explanation. HTML uses the selected drawing provider/model, the latest shared crop,
and the previous HTML panel for follow-up edits. The renderer displays completed
sections as they stream, rather than waiting for the full answer.

HTML supports semantic text, tables, CSS, checkbox/radio controls and expandable
answers (`details`/`summary`). It does **not** execute generated JavaScript, load
remote content, submit forms, navigate, or expose the native bridge. A tag/attribute
allowlist, an iframe sandbox with scripts blocked and a restrictive CSP enforce that boundary. The sandbox allows same-origin access so the page can read positions for the voice's cursor; nothing inside the frame can use that, because no script runs there.
There is one panel, at most eight 8,000-character sections, and a 300ms preview
coalescer. No new WebView or local AI model is created. This is bounded resource
usage by design, not a measured RAM guarantee. Dismiss cancels the in-flight visual;
ending Talk cancels the request and pointer sharing. The API retains the existing
120-second timeout, local-session authentication and two-request concurrency limit.

`/overlay.html?demo=html` is an offline illustrative fixture for layout and answer
expansion checks; it does not start the microphone or an AI request.

### OpenAI spoken replies

Chat replies (including Claude answers) use OpenAI `gpt-4o-mini-tts` instead of the macOS synthesizer. In the expanded notch, choose **••• → Reply voice**: Marin is the default, with Cedar, Coral, Ash and the other built-in voices available. The selection persists. **Preview selected voice** plays a short sample when spoken replies are enabled. The menu identifies the voices as AI-generated. Talk remains a separate Realtime session using Marin; Both comparisons remain silent.

The full text displayed in the floating reply bubble is the speech input, without a second summary or truncation. Long replies are split at whitespace into sequential bounded requests. One MP3 chunk is held in memory at a time, with no persistent audio cache or local model. Muting, dismissing outputs, submitting a new prompt, starting Talk/dictation, changing voice, or quitting cancels pending speech and playback. Failed TTS leaves the text available and reports that voice is unavailable rather than silently changing to a system voice.

Reply text is sent to OpenAI for synthesis using the existing server-side API key. `/api/speech` requires the localhost session token, validates voice/input, admits one synthesis at a time, and cancels on disconnect or after 45 seconds. Test previews for Marin/Cedar, a long reply, mute/dismiss during preparation and playback, and starting Talk while a reply is playing.

### Native backend startup

The app launches Node with `/` as its working directory and an absolute server entry point. Backend files and credentials continue to resolve relative to their modules in the configured checkout. This avoids a macOS startup stall observed in Node's `process.cwd()` resolution when the working directory was the checkout. Verify from a stopped backend: launch Clip, confirm its child process owns port 4317, and confirm `/api/realtime/status` reports the existing voice configuration. The private `.env.local` belongs in that configured checkout and must stay ignored by Git.

### Daily habits

A tiny seven-segment mint ring sits fully inside a reserved part of the same black notch surface, left of the physical camera, in both collapsed and expanded states. The compact shell and expanded camera cover provide its black background; the ring has no separate black backing or exterior attachment. It is a single progress button: clicking it opens or closes a compact checklist for Meditate, Exercise, Stretch, Read, Journal, Math, and Push ups. Only checklist rows change completion; clicking the ring never checks a habit. Outside click, Escape or the list’s close button dismisses the list while the ring remains. Clip → Today’s habits opens it for keyboard access.

Progress is saved locally, resets at local midnight, refreshes after sleep/time-zone changes, and retains at most 90 days. There is one midnight timer, no API calls or animation loop. Clip hides the ring and list during its own screenshots. The ring stays inset while the shell changes during hover/voice activity, with space reserved away from status text and the startup sweep. External displays keep it inside the virtual notch or semicircle.
